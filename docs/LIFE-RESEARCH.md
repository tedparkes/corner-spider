# Life and family design notes

This is a stylized desktop pet, not an identification tool or a spider-care guide.
The 1.1 development build keeps the natural structures recognizable while making
long events observable during ordinary computer use.

- Jumpers keep the species-inspired shelters from 1.0. Orb weavers use spokes
  and capture silk; widows and common house spiders use an irregular tangle;
  barn funnel weavers use a sheet with a funnel opening. Supports join both
  desktop edges. [Australian Museum: spider webs](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/spiders/spider-webs/)
  and [Penn State: common house spider](https://extension.psu.edu/common-house-spider).
- Garden orb weavers detect prey through their web and approach to wrap it.
  Their small eyes and front body differ from the large-eyed jumping spiders.
  [Australian Museum: garden orb weavers](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/spiders/garden-orb-weaving-spiders/).
- Argiope's yellow/black pattern, web decoration and small male informed the
  garden skin; cross and marbled orb-weavers use different abdominal shapes and
  markings. Drawings simplify those markings rather than reproduce photographs.
  [Argiope aurantia](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Argiope_aurantia/),
  [Araneus diadematus](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Araneus_diadematus/),
  [Araneus marmoreus](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Araneus_marmoreus/).
- Molting involves leaving an old cuticle. Repeating adulthood molts followed
  by a gradual return to the previous size is explicitly fictional: real spiders
  do not repeatedly shrink to let an immortal adult grow again. The app uses a
  bounded 16% increase and a smooth return to the user's unchanged size setting.
  [Australian Museum: spiders](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/spiders/).
- Egg sacs and later dispersal are natural inspirations. The six visible young,
  short development times, ongoing parental survival and absence of sibling
  cannibalism are deliberate adaptations. The babies never breed themselves.
  [Australian Museum: egg sacs, spiderlings and dispersal](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/spiders/egg-sacs-spiderlings-and-dispersal/).
- Sexual cannibalism is not inevitable. The southern black widow's male usually
  survives in this model; redback visits have a greater risk. All percentages in
  code are artistic event weights, not measured biological rates. No claim is
  made that every included species has the same mating ritual or egg timing.
  [Burke Museum: black widows eating mates](https://www.burkemuseum.org/collections-and-research/biology/arachnology-and-entomology/spider-myths/myth-black-widows-eat).
- Long-term web reinforcement is a persistent visual history. In nature some
  orb weavers replace capture webs regularly, so accumulated silk in the app
  represents repairs and enduring supports rather than a literal old orb.

Nebula weavers, Crystal spinners and Copper clockwork spiders are original
fictional creatures. Their patterns, silk, molts and family behavior are imagined.
The existing green female Hyllus remains a labeled stylized palette variant.
No downloaded reference photos are included in the application.
